A preá (say *preh-AH*) is a small rodent from South America. In the Brazilian Northeast it is everywhere: in the dry scrubland, at the edge of the roads, running between the cacti.

## Meet the preá

- **Family.** It belongs to the cavy family, Caviidae, the same family as the guinea pig. Despite the size and the whiskers, it is not a rat.
- **The Northeast's own.** The species of the Northeast is *Galea spixii*. It is the most abundant mammal of the **caatinga**, the semi-arid scrubland found only in Brazil.
- **Looks.** A long, round body with a grey-brown back and a light belly, a big head with big dark eyes, and short, round ears.
- **Habits.** It lives in groups, and it is most active at dawn and dusk, when the heat of the day eases. It eats leaves, shoots, fruits and roots of low plants.
- **Born ready.** After about 50 days of pregnancy, a mother has from one to five pups, which are born with fur and with their eyes open.
- **Tough.** It tolerates a wide range of conditions, as long as it has open land, which is why it is so common across its range.

## The word

*Preá* comes from Tupi, one of the native languages of Brazil, where the animal was called *apereá*. Some regions still say *apreá*.

## Why Preá

Preá was created in the Brazilian Northeast, and its authors wanted a name from home. The Go language has a rodent for a mascot, the gopher. A framework for Go got a rodent from the caatinga.

The name also fits the idea. A preá is small, lives in groups and gets by with little. A Preá app is a group of small preás that each do one thing.
